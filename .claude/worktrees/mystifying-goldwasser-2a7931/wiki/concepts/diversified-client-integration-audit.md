# Diversified Client Integration Audit (DIA)

A broader, governance-level audit framework covering the control environment for client integrations — including customer integration controls and licensing/commission process controls.

## Definition

The Diversified Client Integration Audit is an audit framework that evaluates the effectiveness of client integration controls across conversion accuracy, data access controls, and audit/quality processes (sources: `Diversified Client Integration Audit.md.txt`, `DIA Customer Integration Audit - Draft Report.md.txt`, `DIA Licensing and Commission Process Flow and Controls.md.txt`).

## Why It Matters

Where [[cit-audit-control]] governs the specific conversion-audit catalog, DIA governs the wider control environment — who has access to sensitive participant data, how licensing and commission flows are controlled, how conversion risk is mitigated at the program level. It is the framework external auditors and internal audit exercise when they test controls, not individual conversions.

## Evidence / Examples

**Customer Integration Audit** (source: `DIA Customer Integration Audit - Draft Report.md.txt`):

- Evaluates customer integration control environment
- Findings focus on access to sensitive participant data and conversion risk mitigation controls

**Licensing and Commission** (source: `DIA Licensing and Commission Process Flow and Controls.md.txt`):

- Scope covers licensing setup and commission setup/payment
- Controls cover access restrictions, system edits, and approvals

**Focus areas** (source: `Diversified Client Integration Audit.md.txt`):

- Conversion accuracy
- Data access controls
- Audit and quality processes

## Counterarguments

DIA is necessarily high-level: it tests that controls exist and are exercised, not that every individual conversion is perfect. Specific conversion failures are caught by [[cit-audit-control]] and the conversion-level audit catalog; DIA is one layer above.
